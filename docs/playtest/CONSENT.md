# Playtest consent form

The form a participant reads and signs before a session, per §13.6's "screen recorded
**with consent**". Consent is taken before the recording starts. The participant keeps a
copy.

The wording below is deliberately plain. A consent form the participant cannot read has
not obtained consent, and this project's privacy stance (§11.12) is that nothing is
collected in order to avoid needing more — the same rule applies to playtests.

---

## Hohmann Heist — playtest session

**What this is.** *Hohmann Heist* is a free, open-source browser game about orbital
mechanics. You are being asked to play it for about thirty minutes so we can find out
where it is confusing. There is no product to buy and nothing to sign up for.

**We are testing the game, not you.** There is no way to do this wrong. If you get stuck,
that is the single most useful thing that can happen in this session — it tells us
something the game failed to explain.

### What we will record

- **Your screen**, while you play.
- **Your voice**, because we will ask you to say what you are thinking as you go.

We will **not** record your face or your webcam. We will not ask for your name, email
address, date of birth, or any other personal detail, and we ask you not to open anything
on your screen during the session that you would not want recorded.

### What we do with it

The recording is watched by the person running the session, and is used to write a
written summary of what went well and what confused people. That summary is published in
the game's public code repository.

**The summary never names you.** You appear in it as a letter and a number — "P3" — and
nothing in it links that label back to you. If we quote something you said, it is
attributed to that label only.

### How long we keep it

The recording is deleted **within 30 days of the session**, and sooner once the written
summary is finished. It is stored on the facilitator's own device until then, is not
uploaded to any cloud service, and is not shared with anyone else.

We keep the written notes from the session — the summary sheet, without your name on it —
indefinitely, because comparing one round of testing against the next is the point of
running them.

### What you can do

- **Stop at any time.** You do not have to give a reason and nothing happens if you do.
- **Take a break**, or skip anything you would rather not do.
- **Change your mind afterwards.** Tell the facilitator any time before the deletion date
  and the recording is destroyed. You do not have to explain why. Once the written
  summary is published we cannot un-publish it, but as it does not identify you, there is
  nothing in it to remove.
- **Ask anything you like at the end.** During play the facilitator will not answer
  questions — that is deliberate, and it is so that your confusion stays visible rather
  than being smoothed over. Afterwards, they will answer all of them.

### Contact

The facilitator will give you a way to reach them before the session starts. Use it for
anything — a question, a withdrawal, or a request to delete the recording early.
